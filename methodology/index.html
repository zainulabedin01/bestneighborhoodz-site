How we score cities and neighborhoods

Every grade on this site comes from public data and the same simple math. This page explains all of it, so you can judge the scores for yourself.

The short version

  1. We collect 16 measures of daily life from the US Census Bureau and the CDC.
  2. For each measure, we work out where a place sits compared with similar places across the whole US, as a percentile from 0 to 100.
  3. We average those into seven category scores, then into one overall score using the weights below.
  4. The overall score becomes a letter grade. You can change the weights on any ranking page.

Who each place is compared with

A big city and a small suburb are very different places, so we only compare like with like.

  • US cities of 50,000+ people: 876 places.
  • US cities of 10,000 to 50,000 people: 3,321 places.
  • US towns of 1,000 to 10,000 people: 11,466 places.
  • Neighborhoods are compared with every Census block group in the US, about 240,000 small areas.

Categories, measures and weights

CategoryWeightWhat it measures
Cost of living20%Rent, home prices compared with income, and how many renters spend 30% or more of income on rent. Measures: median rent; home value to income; renters paying 30%+ of income on rent.
Jobs and income20%Household income, unemployment and the share of people below the poverty line. Measures: median household income; unemployment rate; people below the poverty line.
Education levels10%Share of adults with a high school diploma and with a bachelor’s degree. Measures: adults with a bachelor's degree or higher; adults with a high school diploma or higher.
Getting around15%Average time to get to work, and how many people walk, bike or take transit. Measures: average commute; workers who walk, bike or take transit.
Home ownership10%Share of homes lived in by their owners, and how many homes sit vacant. Measures: homes owned by the people living in them; vacant homes.
Internet access10%Share of homes with a broadband internet plan. Measures: homes with broadband internet.
Health15%Adults without health insurance, with no exercise outside work, and with frequent mental distress. Measures: adults 18 to 64 without health insurance; adults with no leisure-time exercise; adults with frequent mental distress.

For measures where lower is better, like rent, commute time or poverty, the percentile is flipped so a higher score always means better.

What we never score

We show race and ethnicity, age and households with children on city and neighborhood pages because people ask about them. They are never used in any score or grade. A place is not better or worse because of who lives there.

How neighborhoods are drawn

There is no official map of US neighborhoods. We build them from Census block groups, small areas of roughly 600 to 3,000 people. Each block group inside a city takes the name of the closest neighborhood on the Overture Maps neighborhood layer, if one is within about 1.2 miles. Block groups with no name nearby are grouped by direction, like “Northeast Austin”. Block groups with the same name are combined into one neighborhood.

This means neighborhood edges are approximate and may not match what locals call an area.

Limits of the data

  • Census figures are survey estimates averaged over five years (2020-2024 ACS 5-year). Small areas can have wide error ranges.
  • When a block group median is not published, we use the value for the surrounding Census tract.
  • Neighborhood medians are weighted averages of block group medians, so they are close estimates rather than exact medians.
  • Health figures are CDC model-based estimates at the tract level. The 2025 CDC release has no data for Kentucky or Pennsylvania, so places there are scored without the health category, using the other weights.
  • Census does not publish total commute time for block groups. For neighborhoods we estimate the average from the travel time ranges, using range averages fitted on Census tracts where both are published.
  • Puerto Rico is not included in any comparison yet.
  • We do not yet score crime, schools, weather or walkability. These are coming in a later update.
