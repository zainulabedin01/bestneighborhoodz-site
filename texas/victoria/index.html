What it's like to live in Victoria

Victoria is the anchor city of the Victoria metro area. 66,500 people lived here in 2025, up 1.5% from 2020. It is spread out.

Local spots people know include Riverside Park, Riverside Park Nature Trail and Riverside Park Childrens Playground.

The median age is 35.1, 32% of households have children, 58% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $67,226 a year (14% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Victoria

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,172, 16% lower than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 20 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Victoria

  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 116 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Victoria

Victoria renters pay a median of $1,172 a month, 16% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $47,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 50% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,146 for a one-bedroom, $1,428 for a two-bedroom and $1,701 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $207,100, 27% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,134 before tax, or $1,396 with property tax. That is $224 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Victoria pays $3,144 a year, 26% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Victoria safe?

In 2025, Victoria Police Department reported 241 violent crimes and 1,228 property crimes. That works out to 364 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 6% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,857 property crimes per 100,000, 11% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 34% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Crime rates describe the whole area a police department covers. Some streets are much quieter than the average and some are busier, so look at the neighborhood grades and visit at different times of day.

Schools in Victoria

Victoria has 19 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 47% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Victoria ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Early Childhood Center at Fw Gross and Vickers Elementary. The average rating score across all campuses is 79 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
