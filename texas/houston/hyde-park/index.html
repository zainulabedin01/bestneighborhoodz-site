What it's like to live in Hyde Park

Hyde Park is a neighborhood in Houston with about 7,400 residents, homes built around 1994, a mix of owners and renters and 14% of households with children.

Our grade for Hyde Park is A+. That puts it 23rd of 320 areas we grade in Houston, a city that gets a D+ overall.

Pros of living in Hyde Park

  • Higher incomes. The median household earns $125,200, 93% higher than the Houston median.
  • Well-rated schools nearby. 3 of the 3 closest public schools are rated A or B.
  • Groceries close by. The nearest grocery store is about 0.2 miles from the middle of the neighborhood.
  • Parks within walking distance. The closest park is about 0.2 miles away.

Cons of living in Hyde Park

  • Pricier rent than the city. Median rent is $1,780, 31% higher than the Houston median.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes as very high in this part of town.

Questions about Hyde Park

Is Hyde Park a good place to live?

Hyde Park is a neighborhood in Houston, TX, home to about 7,400 people. It gets an A+, in the top 5% of Texas neighborhoods, and ranks 23rd of 320 areas we score in Houston. It does best on schools and worst on home ownership.

How much does it cost to live in Hyde Park?

Renters in Hyde Park pay a median of $1,780 a month, 31% higher than the Houston median. The median home is worth $587,000, 111% higher than the Houston median. A median home costs 4.7x the median household income, compared with 4.3x across Houston.

What schools serve Hyde Park?

The closest rated public schools are Wharton K-8 Dual Language Academy (elementary, rated B), Wharton K-8 Dual Language Academy (middle, rated B) and Carnegie Vanguard High School (high, rated A). School zones can differ from the closest school, so check with the district.
