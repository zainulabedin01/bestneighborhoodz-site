What it's like to live in Universal City

Universal City sits about 14 miles from downtown San Antonio, in the San Antonio-New Braunfels metro. 20,399 people lived here in 2025, up 3.5% from 2020. It is suburban.

Local spots people know include Veterans park, Universal City Park and Red Horse Park.

The median age is 37.1, 29% of households have children, 57% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $74,811 a year (5% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Universal City

  • Low violent crime. 167 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 57% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Plenty close to home. 5 grocery stores, 86 restaurants and 14 parks are mapped in town.

Cons of living in Universal City

  • Weaker school ratings. None of the 4 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of river flooding, tornadoes and heat waves as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 122 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Universal City

Universal City renters pay a median of $1,315 a month, 6% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $53,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 39% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,196 for a one-bedroom, $1,441 for a two-bedroom and $1,864 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $269,600, 5% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,476 before tax, or $1,905 with property tax. That is $590 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Universal City pays $5,147 a year, 22% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
