What it's like to live in Texarkana

Texarkana is the anchor city of the Texarkana, TX-AR metro area. 36,195 people lived here in 2025, down 0.1% from 2020. It is spread out.

Local spots people know include Spring Lake Park Splash Pad, Spring Lake Park and New Bringle Park.

The median age is 37.2, 28% of households have children, 52% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $50,573 a year (36% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Texarkana

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,010, 28% lower than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 15 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Texarkana

  • Weaker school ratings. Only 33% of the 15 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Higher violent crime. 652 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 68% higher than the Texas rate.
  • Higher poverty. 22% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, heat waves and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Texarkana

Texarkana renters pay a median of $1,010 a month, 28% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $40,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 57% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $874 for a one-bedroom, $1,118 for a two-bedroom and $1,403 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $171,500, 40% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $939 before tax, or $1,137 with property tax. That is $127 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Texarkana pays $2,379 a year, 44% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Texarkana safe?

In 2025, Texarkana Police Department reported 234 violent crimes and 1,018 property crimes. That works out to 652 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 68% higher than the Texas rate of 387, and 2,835 property crimes per 100,000, 36% higher than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 12% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Schools in Texarkana

Texarkana has 15 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 33% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Texarkana ISD and Pleasant Grove ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Martha & Josh Morriss Math & Engineering Elementary, Pleasant Grove High School and Pleasant Grove Middle. The average rating score across all campuses is 73 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.

    Living in Texarkana: common questions

    Is Texarkana, TX a good place to live?

    Renters in Texarkana pay a median of $1,010 a month, 28% lower than the Texas median. The median home is worth $171,500, 40% lower than the Texas median.
