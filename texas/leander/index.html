What it's like to live in Leander

Leander sits about 20 miles from downtown Austin, in the Austin-Round Rock-San Marcos metro. 91,132 people lived here in 2025, up 53.9% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is spread out, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2013).

Local spots people know include Lakewood Park, Devine Lake Park and Benbrook Ranch Park.

The median age is 36.3, 45% of households have children, 73% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $139,048 a year (77% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Leander

  • Strong public schools. 75% of the 20 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 58 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 85% lower than the Texas rate.
  • High incomes. The median household earns $139,048, 77% higher than the Texas median.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 54% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Leander

  • Higher rent. Median rent is $1,939, 38% higher than the Texas median.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hail as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 112 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Leander

Leander renters pay a median of $1,939 a month, 38% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $78,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 46% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,530 for a one-bedroom, $1,817 for a two-bedroom and $2,292 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $506,200, 78% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $2,771 before tax, or $3,528 with property tax. That is $1,589 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Leander pays $9,083 a year, 115% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Leander safe?

In 2025, Leander Police Department reported 56 violent crimes and 451 property crimes. That works out to 58 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 85% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 471 property crimes per 100,000, 77% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 28% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Schools in Leander

Leander has 20 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 75% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Leander ISD, Texas College Preparatory Academies and Valor Education.

Top-rated campuses include Florence W Stiles Middle, Early College High School and Founders Classical Academy of Leander. The average rating score across all campuses is 86 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
