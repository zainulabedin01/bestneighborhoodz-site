What it's like to live in Whitehouse

Whitehouse sits about 8 miles from downtown Tyler, in the Tyler metro. 9,970 people lived here in 2025, up 21.1% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is spread out.

Local spots people know include Whitehouse Community Library and Whitehouse Splash Park.

The median age is 32.4, 43% of households have children, 75% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $99,458 a year (27% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Whitehouse

  • Strong public schools. 100% of the 6 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 59 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 85% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.6 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • High incomes. The median household earns $99,458, 27% higher than the Texas median.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 21% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Whitehouse

  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of heat waves, wildfires and strong wind as very high here.

Cost of living in Whitehouse

Whitehouse renters pay a median of $1,458 a month, about the same as the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $58,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 39% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,041 for a one-bedroom, $1,277 for a two-bedroom and $1,718 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $253,800, 11% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,389 before tax, or $1,731 with property tax. That is $273 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Whitehouse pays $4,104 a year, about the same as the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Schools in Whitehouse

Whitehouse has 6 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 100% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Whitehouse ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Stanton-Smith Elementary, Gus Winston Cain Elementary and Whitehouse Junior High. The average rating score across all campuses is 91 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Daily life in Whitehouse

Everyday places mapped inside Whitehouse, from Overture Maps. Well-known spots include Whitehouse Community Library, Whitehouse Splash Park.

The average one-way commute is 28 minutes, about the same as the Texas average. 15% of workers work from home. Fewer than 3% walk, bike or take transit to work, so plan on driving for almost everything.

Downtown Tyler is about 8 miles away in a straight line. Many people who live in Whitehouse work elsewhere in the metro, so test the drive at rush hour before you commit.

The median household income is $99,458, income per person is $37,380, unemployment is 6.4% and 33% of adults hold a bachelor's degree.
