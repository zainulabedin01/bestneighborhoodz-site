What it's like to live in Memphis

Memphis is a town in Hall County. 1,969 people lived here in 2025, down 3.7% from 2020. It is spread out, with an older housing stock (the median home dates to 1962).

The median age is 40.6, 35% of households have children, 73% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $47,639 a year (39% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Memphis

  • Strong public schools. 100% of the 3 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 196 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 49% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $491, 65% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 1.6 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 14 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Memphis

  • Higher unemployment. 10% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Spotty home internet. Only 78% of homes have a broadband plan.

Cost of living in Memphis

Memphis renters pay a median of $491 a month, 65% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $20,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 34% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $794 for a one-bedroom, $1,014 for a two-bedroom and $1,404 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $77,300, 73% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $423 before tax, or $510 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Memphis pays $1,045 a year, 75% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
