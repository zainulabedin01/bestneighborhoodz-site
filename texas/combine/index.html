What it's like to live in Combine

Combine sits about 21 miles from downtown Dallas, in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro. 2,769 people lived here in 2025, up 24.9% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is small-town and rural in feel.

The median age is 38.7, 40% of households have children, 91% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $94,756 a year (21% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Combine

  • Growing fast. The population grew 25% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Combine

  • Higher rent. Median rent is $1,741, 24% higher than the Texas median.
  • Higher unemployment. 10% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Long commutes. The average trip to work takes 39 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cost of living in Combine

Combine renters pay a median of $1,741 a month, 24% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $70,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 8% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,536 for a one-bedroom, $1,807 for a two-bedroom and $2,275 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $323,800, 14% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,772 before tax, or $2,126 with property tax. That is $385 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Combine pays $4,244 a year, about the same as the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
