What it's like to live in Mansfield

Mansfield sits about 26 miles from downtown Dallas, in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro. 84,444 people lived here in 2025, up 16.3% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is suburban, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2004).

Local spots people know include Britton Park, James McKnight Park and Katherine Rose Memorial Park.

The median age is 36.4, 43% of households have children, 72% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $121,126 a year (54% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Mansfield

  • Strong public schools. 89% of the 27 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 106 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 73% lower than the Texas rate.
  • High incomes. The median household earns $121,126, 54% higher than the Texas median.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 16% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Mansfield

  • Higher rent. Median rent is $1,954, 39% higher than the Texas median.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, heat waves and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Mansfield

Mansfield renters pay a median of $1,954 a month, 39% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $78,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 56% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,481 for a one-bedroom, $1,745 for a two-bedroom and $2,284 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $410,700, 45% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $2,248 before tax, or $2,885 with property tax. That is $931 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Mansfield pays $7,645 a year, 81% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Mansfield safe?

In 2025, Mansfield Police Department reported 88 violent crimes and 986 property crimes. That works out to 106 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 73% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,189 property crimes per 100,000, 43% lower than the state rate.

Schools in Mansfield

Mansfield has 27 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 89% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Mansfield ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Mansfield Frontier STEM Academy, Mansfield Lake Ridge High School and Mansfield High School. The average rating score across all campuses is 88 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
