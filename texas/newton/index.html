What it's like to live in Newton

Newton is a town in Newton County. 1,595 people lived here in 2025, down 3.8% from 2020. It is small-town and rural in feel.

Local spots people know include Caney Creek Park, Newton Park and Newton Public Library.

The median age is 37.2, 38% of households have children, 66% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $56,172 a year (28% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Newton

  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 1.8 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.

Cons of living in Newton

Cost of living in Newton

Newton renters pay a median of $1,032 a month, 26% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $41,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 51% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $807 for a one-bedroom, $1,014 for a two-bedroom and $1,404 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $102,100, 64% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $559 before tax, or $640 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Newton pays $976 a year, 77% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
