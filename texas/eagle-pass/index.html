What it's like to live in Eagle Pass

Eagle Pass is the anchor city of the Eagle Pass metro area. 28,926 people lived here in 2025, up 2.5% from 2020. It is suburban.

Places locals use include Eagle Pass Lake, Jesse Sumpter Park and Plaza San Juan.

The median age is 30.0, 42% of households have children, 60% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $57,207 a year (27% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Eagle Pass

  • Low violent crime. 180 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 54% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $880, 37% lower than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 20 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Plenty close to home. 11 grocery stores, 145 restaurants and 24 parks are mapped in town.

Cons of living in Eagle Pass

  • Higher poverty. 22% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Many adults uninsured. 35% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of heat waves as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 151 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Eagle Pass

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Eagle Pass is $880 a month, 37% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $35,000 a year. Right now 48% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $931 for a one-bedroom, $1,014 for a two-bedroom and $1,404 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $188,000, 34% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,029 before tax, or $1,288 with property tax. That is $408 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Eagle Pass pays $3,111 a year, 26% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Eagle Pass safe?

In 2025, Eagle Pass Police Department reported 52 violent crimes and 442 property crimes. That works out to 180 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 54% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,528 property crimes per 100,000, 27% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime has been fairly steady since 2021.

Crime rates describe the whole area a police department covers. Some streets are much quieter than the average and some are busier, so look at the neighborhood grades and visit at different times of day.
