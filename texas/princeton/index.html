What it's like to live in Princeton

Princeton sits about 31 miles from downtown Dallas, in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro. 43,524 people lived here in 2025, up 156.1% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is spread out, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2014).

Places locals use include J.M. Caldwell Sr. Community Park, Trails at Riverstone Park and Princeton Dog Park.

The median age is 32.4, 52% of households have children, 76% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $105,200 a year (34% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Princeton

  • High incomes. The median household earns $105,200, 34% higher than the Texas median.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 156% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Princeton

  • Higher rent. Median rent is $2,239, 60% higher than the Texas median.
  • Long commutes. The average trip to work takes 32 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, heat waves and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Princeton

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Princeton is $2,239 a month, 60% higher than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $90,000 a year. Right now 42% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,536 for a one-bedroom, $1,807 for a two-bedroom and $2,275 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $331,000, 17% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,812 before tax, or $2,256 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Princeton pays $5,326 a year, 26% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Schools in Princeton

Princeton has 10 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 70% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Princeton ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Clark Middle and Harper Elementary. The average rating score across all campuses is 82 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.

Daily life in Princeton

Everyday places mapped inside Princeton, from Overture Maps. Well-known spots include J.M. Caldwell Sr. Community Park, Trails at Riverstone Park, Princeton Dog Park.

The average one-way commute is 32 minutes, 21% longer than the Texas average. 15% of workers work from home. Fewer than 3% walk, bike or take transit to work, so plan on driving for almost everything.

Downtown Dallas is about 31 miles away in a straight line. Many people who live in Princeton work elsewhere in the metro, so test the drive at rush hour before you commit.

The median household income is $105,200, income per person is $37,704, unemployment is 2.5% and 29% of adults hold a bachelor's degree.

Living in Princeton: common questions

Is Princeton, TX a good place to live?

How much does it cost to live in Princeton?
