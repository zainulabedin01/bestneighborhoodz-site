What it's like to live in Jasper

Jasper is a town in Jasper County. 7,511 people lived here in 2025, up 9.3% from 2020. It is small-town and rural in feel.

Local spots people know include Sandy Creek Park, Jasper Butterfly Park Arboretum and Carridine Park.

The median age is 38.3, 30% of households have children, 57% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $45,078 a year (43% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Jasper

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $892, 36% lower than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 22 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Jasper

  • Higher unemployment. 9.3% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Higher poverty. 27% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes, tornadoes and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Jasper

Jasper renters pay a median of $892 a month, 36% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $36,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 61% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $905 for a one-bedroom, $1,014 for a two-bedroom and $1,380 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $130,100, 54% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $712 before tax, or $819 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Jasper pays $1,279 a year, 70% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

In 2025, Jasper Police Department reported 33 violent crimes and 202 property crimes. That works out to 430 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 11% higher than the Texas rate of 387, and 2,635 property crimes per 100,000, 26% higher than the state rate.
