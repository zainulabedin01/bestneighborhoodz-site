What it's like to live in Indian Hills

Indian Hills sits about 20 miles from downtown McAllen, in the McAllen-Edinburg-Mission metro. About 2,961 people live here. It is spread out, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2001).

The median age is 23.0, 60% of households have children, 96% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $32,816 a year (58% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Indian Hills

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,155, 18% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 1.7 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 23 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Indian Hills

  • Higher poverty. 67% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Many adults uninsured. 51% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 160 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Indian Hills

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Indian Hills is $1,155 a month, 18% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $46,000 a year. Right now 81% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $806 for a one-bedroom, $1,017 for a two-bedroom and $1,305 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $54,400, 81% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $298 before tax, or $358 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Indian Hills pays $723 a year, 83% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
