What it's like to live in Big Lake

Big Lake is a town in Reagan County. 2,783 people lived here in 2025, down 7.5% from 2020. It is spread out.

Places locals use include Reagan County Park, Josh Mitchell Park and Bertrand Park.

The median age is 27.9, 55% of households have children, 75% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $71,471 a year (9% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Big Lake

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $746, 47% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.2 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 19 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Big Lake

Cost of living in Big Lake

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Big Lake is $746 a month, 47% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $30,000 a year. Right now 14% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $807 for a one-bedroom, $1,014 for a two-bedroom and $1,310 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $157,900, 44% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $864 before tax, or $1,002 with property tax. That is $256 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Big Lake pays $1,657 a year, 61% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
