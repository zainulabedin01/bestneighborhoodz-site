What it's like to live in Troy

Troy sits about 27 miles from downtown Killeen, in the Killeen-Temple metro. 3,585 people lived here in 2025, up 51.0% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is small-town and rural in feel, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2001).

The median age is 33.8, 45% of households have children, 65% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $86,311 a year (10% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Troy

  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.3 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 51% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Troy

  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, hail and wildfires as very high here.

Cost of living in Troy

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Troy is $1,324 a month, 6% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $53,000 a year. Right now 31% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $927 for a one-bedroom, $1,138 for a two-bedroom and $1,576 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $201,500, 29% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,103 before tax, or $1,346 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Troy pays $2,914 a year, 31% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Living in Troy: common questions

Is Troy, TX a good place to live?

Troy gets an overall grade of B and ranks 220th of 745 Texas towns of 1,000 to 10,000 people. It does best on jobs and income and cost of living, and trails on getting around and health.

How much does it cost to live in Troy?

Median rent in Troy is $1,324 a month, 6% lower than the Texas median. The median home is worth $201,500, 29% lower than the Texas median. A median home costs 2.3x the median household income, compared with 3.6x across Texas. With 20% down at a 7.28% rate, the monthly payment on a median home is about $1,346, including property tax.

Is Troy safe?

In 2025, Troy Police Department reported 53 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 86% lower than the Texas rate. Property crime was 134 per 100,000, 94% lower than the Texas rate. This agency reported only part of the year, so the rate is shown but not scored.

Are the schools good in Troy?

We found 4 rated public school campuses inside Troy. 100% are rated A or B by the Texas Education Agency. Its schools score is 66 of 100 compared with other Texas towns of 1,000 to 10,000 people.

Does Troy flood?

FEMA rates river flooding risk in Troy as moderate, and coastal flooding as not rated. Hazards rated high or very high here: hurricanes, tornadoes, heat waves, hail, wildfires and winter storms. Always check the flood map for a specific address before you buy.

What is the weather like in Troy?

Summers in Troy peak at about 95°F in July, and January nights fall to about 34°F. Expect around 94 days a year at 90°F or hotter, and 38 inches of rain.

How long is the commute in Troy?

The average one-way commute is 27 min, about the same as the Texas average. About 2.4% of workers work from home. It is about 27 miles from downtown Killeen.
