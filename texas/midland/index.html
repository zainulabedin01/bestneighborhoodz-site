About Midland

Out in the Permian Basin of West Texas, Midland got its name from where it sits. It began in 1881 as Midway Station on the Texas and Pacific Railway, halfway between Fort Worth and El Paso, and took the name Midland in 1884. It became the seat of Midland County a year later. Odessa is its neighbor to the west.

Oil shapes almost everything here. After the Santa Rita No. 1 well came in 1923, Midland became the office town for the West Texas oil fields, and the Permian Basin is still one of the biggest oil and gas regions in the country. Interstate 20 runs along the south side of town, and Loop 250 wraps around the west, north and east edges. Large employers include Midland ISD, Midland Memorial Hospital and Dawson Geophysical. Midland International Air and Space Port sits between Midland and Odessa. The Permian Basin Petroleum Museum, the Museum of the Southwest and the Bush family childhood home are the best known stops in town.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org, en.wikipedia.org

What it's like to live in Midland

Midland is the anchor city of the Midland metro area. 147,615 people lived here in 2025, up 11.1% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is spread out.

Local spots people know include Windlands Park, Hogan Park and Dunagan Park.

The median age is 31.5, 40% of households have children, 65% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $89,585 a year (14% higher than the Texas median).

  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 20 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Midland

  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, hail and winter storms as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 126 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Midland

Midland renters pay a median of $1,434 a month, about the same as the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $57,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 53% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,408 for a one-bedroom, $1,794 for a two-bedroom and $2,223 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $319,200, 12% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,747 before tax, or $2,086 with property tax. That is $652 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Midland pays $4,067 a year, about the same as the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Midland safe?

In 2025, Midland Police Department reported 516 violent crimes and 2,388 property crimes. That works out to 352 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 9% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,631 property crimes per 100,000, 22% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime has been fairly steady since 2021.

Crime rates describe the whole area a police department covers. Some streets are much quieter than the average and some are busier, so look at the neighborhood grades and visit at different times of day.

Schools in Midland

Midland has 41 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 37% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Midland ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Early College H S at Midland College, Carver Center and Young Women's Leadership Academy. The average rating score across all campuses is 77 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
