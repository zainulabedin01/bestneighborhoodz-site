What it's like to live in Ozona

Ozona is a town in Crockett County. About 2,405 people live here. It is small-town and rural in feel.

Local spots people know include Crockett County Fair Park, Ozona Baseball Park and Crockett County Park.

The median age is 45.6, 35% of households have children, 77% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $81,622 a year (4% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Ozona

  • Strong public schools. 100% of the 3 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $558, 60% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 0.8 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 20 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Ozona

Cost of living in Ozona

Ozona renters pay a median of $558 a month, 60% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $22,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 0% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $858 for a one-bedroom, $1,078 for a two-bedroom and $1,393 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $66,900, 76% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $366 before tax, or $393 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Ozona pays $317 a year, 93% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
