What it's like to live in Big Spring

Big Spring is a small city in Howard County. 22,290 people lived here in 2025, down 15.3% from 2020. It is spread out, with an older housing stock (the median home dates to 1963).

Places locals use include Comanche Trail Park, ABC Park and Birdwell Park.

The median age is 36.4, 35% of households have children, 65% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $67,581 a year (14% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Big Spring

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,050, 25% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.1 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 21 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Big Spring

  • Higher violent crime. 860 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 122% higher than the Texas rate.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Many adults uninsured. 29% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hail, wildfires and winter storms as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 115 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Big Spring

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Big Spring is $1,050 a month, 25% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $42,000 a year. Right now 53% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $954 for a one-bedroom, $1,217 for a two-bedroom and $1,492 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $140,200, 51% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $767 before tax, or $886 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Big Spring pays $1,420 a year, 66% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Big Spring safe?

In 2025, Big Spring Police Department reported 187 violent crimes and 532 property crimes. That works out to 860 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 122% higher than the Texas rate of 387, and 2,448 property crimes per 100,000, 17% higher than the state rate.
