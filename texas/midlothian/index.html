What it's like to live in Midlothian

Midlothian sits about 26 miles from downtown Dallas, in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro. 46,683 people lived here in 2025, up 33.1% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is small-town and rural in feel, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2007).

Local spots people know include Midlothian Community Park, Mountain Peak Community Park and Jaycee Park.

The median age is 36.7, 46% of households have children, 80% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $126,641 a year (61% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Midlothian

  • Low violent crime. 99 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 74% lower than the Texas rate.
  • High incomes. The median household earns $126,641, 61% higher than the Texas median.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 33% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Midlothian

  • Higher rent. Median rent is $2,013, 43% higher than the Texas median.
  • Long commutes. The average trip to work takes 32 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, heat waves and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Midlothian

Midlothian renters pay a median of $2,013 a month, 43% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $81,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 50% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,536 for a one-bedroom, $1,807 for a two-bedroom and $2,275 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $411,800, 45% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $2,254 before tax, or $2,786 with property tax. That is $773 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Midlothian pays $6,386 a year, 51% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Midlothian safe?

In 2025, Midlothian Police Department reported 46 violent crimes and 404 property crimes. That works out to 99 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 74% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 868 property crimes per 100,000, 58% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime has been fairly steady since 2021.

Crime rates describe the whole area a police department covers. Some streets are much quieter than the average and some are busier, so look at the neighborhood grades and visit at different times of day.

Schools in Midlothian

Midlothian has 12 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 83% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Midlothian ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Walnut Grove Middle. The average rating score across all campuses is 85 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.

    Living in Midlothian: common questions
