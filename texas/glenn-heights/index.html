What it's like to live in Glenn Heights

Glenn Heights sits about 18 miles from downtown Dallas, in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro. 20,327 people lived here in 2025, up 28.2% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is suburban, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2004).

The median age is 33.8, 40% of households have children, 78% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $81,718 a year (4% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Glenn Heights

  • Low violent crime. 221 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 43% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 28% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Glenn Heights

  • Weaker school ratings. Only 25% of the 4 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Higher rent. Median rent is $1,675, 19% higher than the Texas median.
  • Long commutes. The average trip to work takes 33 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, heat waves and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Glenn Heights

Glenn Heights renters pay a median of $1,675 a month, 19% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $67,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 71% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,536 for a one-bedroom, $1,807 for a two-bedroom and $2,275 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $309,600, 9% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,695 before tax, or $2,085 with property tax. That is $410 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Glenn Heights pays $4,680 a year, 11% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
