What it's like to live in League City

League City sits about 27 miles from downtown Houston, in the Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro. 119,905 people lived here in 2025, up 4.9% from 2020. It is suburban, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2003).

Local spots people know include Challenger 7 Memorial Park, Hometown Heroes Park and Walter Hall Park.

The median age is 37.7, 37% of households have children, 75% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $121,099 a year (54% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in League City

  • Strong public schools. 96% of the 23 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 60 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 84% lower than the Texas rate.
  • High incomes. The median household earns $121,099, 54% higher than the Texas median.

Cons of living in League City

  • Higher rent. Median rent is $1,684, 20% higher than the Texas median.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes as very high here.

Cost of living in League City

League City renters pay a median of $1,684 a month, 20% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $67,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 51% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,261 for a one-bedroom, $1,497 for a two-bedroom and $2,017 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $360,000, 27% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,971 before tax, or $2,459 with property tax. That is $775 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in League City pays $5,864 a year, 39% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is League City safe?

In 2025, League City Police Department reported 72 violent crimes and 1,280 property crimes. That works out to 60 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 84% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,072 property crimes per 100,000, 49% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 42% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Schools in League City

League City has 23 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 96% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Clear Creek ISD and Dickinson ISD.

Top-rated campuses include League City Intermediate, Ralph Parr Elementary and Bay Colony Elementary. The average rating score across all campuses is 87 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
