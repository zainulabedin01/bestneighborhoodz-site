What it's like to live in Cooper

Places locals use include Delta County Public Library and Cooper City Park.

Cost of living in Cooper

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Cooper is $877 a month, 37% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $35,000 a year. Right now 71% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $839 for a one-bedroom, $1,014 for a two-bedroom and $1,404 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $100,500, 65% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $550 before tax, or $693 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Cooper pays $1,719 a year, 59% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
