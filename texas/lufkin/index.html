What it's like to live in Lufkin

Lufkin is a small city in Angelina County. 34,350 people lived here in 2025, up 0.5% from 2020. It is spread out.

Local spots people know include Morris Frank Park, Grace Dunne Richardson Park and Kit McConnico Park.

The median age is 34.6, 34% of households have children, 52% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $57,048 a year (27% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Lufkin

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,079, 23% lower than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 17 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Lufkin

  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of heat waves and strong wind as very high here.

Cost of living in Lufkin

Lufkin renters pay a median of $1,079 a month, 23% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $43,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 53% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $944 for a one-bedroom, $1,073 for a two-bedroom and $1,464 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $179,600, 37% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $983 before tax, or $1,196 with property tax. That is $117 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Lufkin pays $2,559 a year, 40% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Lufkin safe?

In 2025, Lufkin Police Department reported 152 violent crimes and 720 property crimes. That works out to 441 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 14% higher than the Texas rate of 387, and 2,089 property crimes per 100,000, about the same as the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 35% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.
