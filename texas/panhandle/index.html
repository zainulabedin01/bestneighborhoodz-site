What it's like to live in Panhandle

Panhandle sits about 28 miles from downtown Amarillo, in the Amarillo metro. 2,288 people lived here in 2025, down 3.4% from 2020. It is spread out, with an older housing stock (the median home dates to 1958).

Local spots people know include Rotary Park and Carson County Public Library.

The median age is 48.2, 20% of households have children, 77% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $85,870 a year (9% higher than the Texas median).

  • Strong public schools. 100% of the 3 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 0 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 100% lower than the Texas rate.

  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 1.9 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.

Cost of living in Panhandle

Panhandle renters pay a median of $1,038 a month, 26% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $42,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 32% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $904 for a one-bedroom, $1,107 for a two-bedroom and $1,512 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $161,000, 43% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $881 before tax, or $1,044 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Panhandle pays $1,957 a year, 54% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
