What it's like to live in Del Rio

Del Rio is a small city in Val Verde County. 34,496 people lived here in 2025, down 0.6% from 2020. It is spread out.

Places locals use include Buena Vista Park, Moore Park and Blue hole.

The median age is 32.8, 41% of households have children, 69% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $66,158 a year (16% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Del Rio

  • Low violent crime. 147 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 62% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $895, 36% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.4 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 19 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Del Rio

  • Many adults uninsured. 33% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hail and winter storms as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 141 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Del Rio

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Del Rio is $895 a month, 36% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $36,000 a year. Right now 49% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $924 for a one-bedroom, $1,119 for a two-bedroom and $1,485 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $160,400, 43% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $878 before tax, or $1,083 with property tax. That is $188 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Del Rio pays $2,455 a year, 42% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Del Rio safe?

In 2025, Del Rio Police Department reported 51 violent crimes and 342 property crimes. That works out to 147 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 62% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 987 property crimes per 100,000, 53% lower than the state rate.

Schools in Del Rio

Del Rio has 11 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 46% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by San Felipe-Del Rio CISD.

Top-rated campuses include Del Rio Early College High School. The average rating score across all campuses is 82 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
