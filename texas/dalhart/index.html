What it's like to live in Dalhart

Dalhart is a town in Dallam County. 8,377 people lived here in 2025, down 0.6% from 2020. It is spread out.

Places locals use include Rita Blanca Lake Center, Veterans Memorial Park and Swimming Pool Park.

The median age is 30.9, 34% of households have children, 65% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $71,630 a year (9% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Dalhart

  • Low violent crime. 71 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 82% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,069, 24% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.3 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 13 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Plenty close to home. 4 grocery stores, 42 restaurants and 7 parks are mapped in town.

Cons of living in Dalhart

  • Weaker school ratings. Only 25% of the 4 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Many adults uninsured. 26% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, hail and wildfires as very high here.

Cost of living in Dalhart

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Dalhart is $1,069 a month, 24% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $43,000 a year. Right now 40% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,099 for a one-bedroom, $1,381 for a two-bedroom and $1,645 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $161,600, 43% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $885 before tax, or $1,058 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Dalhart pays $2,082 a year, 51% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
