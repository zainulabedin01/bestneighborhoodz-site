What it's like to live in San Elizario

San Elizario sits about 21 miles from downtown El Paso, in the El Paso metro. 10,138 people lived here in 2025, up 0.1% from 2020. It is spread out, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2001).

Places locals use include Alejandra Flores Park and Ted Richardson Park.

The median age is 31.1, 56% of households have children, 68% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $49,977 a year (36% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in San Elizario

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $917, 35% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.2 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.

Cons of living in San Elizario

  • Weaker school ratings. Only 17% of the 6 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Higher poverty. 26% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Long commutes. The average trip to work takes 34 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Many adults uninsured. 41% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.

Cost of living in San Elizario

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in San Elizario is $917 a month, 35% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $37,000 a year. Right now 31% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $969 for a one-bedroom, $1,146 for a two-bedroom and $1,559 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $110,400, 61% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $604 before tax, or $752 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in San Elizario pays $1,769 a year, 58% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
