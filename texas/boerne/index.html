What it's like to live in Boerne

Boerne sits about 25 miles from downtown San Antonio, in the San Antonio-New Braunfels metro. 24,047 people lived here in 2025, up 34.6% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is spread out, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2006).

Local spots people know include Boerne City Park, Northrup Park and Boerne City Lake Park.

The median age is 38.7, 40% of households have children, 65% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $84,541 a year (8% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Boerne

  • Strong public schools. 100% of the 8 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 112 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 71% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Plenty close to home. 4 grocery stores, 128 restaurants and 19 parks are mapped in town.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 35% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Boerne

  • Homes are pricey for local incomes. The median home costs 5.4 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of river flooding, tornadoes and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Boerne

Boerne renters pay a median of $1,589 a month, 13% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $64,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 55% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,285 for a one-bedroom, $1,650 for a two-bedroom and $2,285 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $457,900, 61% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $2,506 before tax, or $3,026 with property tax. That is $1,437 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Boerne pays $6,232 a year, 47% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Schools in Boerne

Top-rated campuses include Boerne - Samuel V Champion High School, Boerne High School and Boerne Middle South. The average rating score across all campuses is 91 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Daily life in Boerne

Everyday places mapped inside Boerne, from Overture Maps. Well-known spots include Boerne City Park, Northrup Park, Boerne City Lake Park.

The average one-way commute is 25 minutes, 5% shorter than the Texas average. 16% of workers work from home. Fewer than 3% walk, bike or take transit to work, so plan on driving for almost everything.

Downtown San Antonio is about 25 miles away in a straight line. Many people who live in Boerne work elsewhere in the metro, so test the drive at rush hour before you commit.

The median household income is $84,541, income per person is $46,343, unemployment is 3.5% and 48% of adults hold a bachelor's degree.
