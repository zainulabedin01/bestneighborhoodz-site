About Grand Prairie

Right in the middle of the Metroplex, Grand Prairie runs from about 12 miles west of downtown Dallas to about 15 miles east of Fort Worth. Most of the city is in Dallas County, with parts in Tarrant and Ellis counties. It started in 1863 as Dechman, named for Alexander McRae Dechman, and took its current name from old maps that labeled this region the grand prairie of Texas. It incorporated in 1909.

I-30 and I-20 run east and west across the city, and SH 360 and SH 161 (the President George Bush Turnpike) run north and south. Aerospace and manufacturing are a big deal here. Lockheed Martin Missiles and Fire Control is the largest employer, Airbus Helicopters has its U.S. headquarters in town, and Poly-America runs a large plant. On the south side, Joe Pool Lake draws people for fishing, boating and swimming, and Lone Star Park hosts horse racing.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org, gptx.org

What it's like to live in Grand Prairie

Grand Prairie sits about 16 miles from downtown Dallas, in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro. 209,434 people lived here in 2025, up 6.8% from 2020.

Local spots people know include Lynn Creek Park at Joe Pool Lake, Lloyd Park Campground and Mountain Creek Lake Park.

The median age is 34.4, 38% of households have children, 59% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $81,619 a year (4% higher than the Texas median).

Cost of living in Grand Prairie

Grand Prairie renters pay a median of $1,629 a month, 16% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $65,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 59% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,536 for a one-bedroom, $1,807 for a two-bedroom and $2,275 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $295,500, 4% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,617 before tax, or $2,030 with property tax. That is $401 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Grand Prairie pays $4,954 a year, 17% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Grand Prairie safe?

In 2025, Grand Prairie Police Department reported 424 violent crimes and 2,796 property crimes. That works out to 202 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 48% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,331 property crimes per 100,000, 36% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 14% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Schools in Grand Prairie

Grand Prairie has 57 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 40% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Mansfield ISD and Grand Prairie ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Cora Spencer Elementary, Grand Prairie Collegiate Institute and Grand Prairie Fine Arts Academy. The average rating score across all campuses is 77 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.

    Living in Grand Prairie: common questions

    Is Grand Prairie, TX a good place to live?

    We give Grand Prairie a D. It ranks 65th of 75 Texas cities of 50,000+ people. It does best on home ownership and safety, and trails on getting around and health.

    Renters in Grand Prairie pay a median of $1,629 a month, 16% higher than the Texas median. The median home is worth $295,500, 4% higher than the Texas median.
