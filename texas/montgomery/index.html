What it's like to live in Montgomery

Montgomery sits about 46 miles from downtown Houston, in the Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro. 3,105 people lived here in 2025, up 60.3% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is small-town and rural in feel, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2013).

Local spots people know include Cedar Brake Park, Memory Park and Fernland Historical Park.

The median age is 35.4, 28% of households have children, 54% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $72,917 a year (7% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Montgomery

  • Strong public schools. 67% of the 3 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 192 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 50% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 60% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Montgomery

  • Homes are pricey for local incomes. The median home costs 5.0 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Long commutes. The average trip to work takes 38 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cost of living in Montgomery

Montgomery renters pay a median of $1,417 a month, about the same as the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $57,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 68% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,261 for a one-bedroom, $1,497 for a two-bedroom and $2,017 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $363,200, 28% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,988 before tax, or $2,415 with property tax. That is $998 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Montgomery pays $5,124 a year, 21% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Daily life in Montgomery

Everyday places mapped inside Montgomery, from Overture Maps. Well-known spots include Cedar Brake Park, Memory Park, Fernland Historical Park.

The average one-way commute is 38 minutes, 42% longer than the Texas average. 8% of workers work from home. Fewer than 3% walk, bike or take transit to work, so plan on driving for almost everything.

Downtown Houston is about 46 miles away in a straight line. Many people who live in Montgomery work elsewhere in the metro, so test the drive at rush hour before you commit.

The median household income is $72,917, income per person is $38,865, unemployment is 7.2% and 35% of adults hold a bachelor's degree.
