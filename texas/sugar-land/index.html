About Sugar Land

Sugar Land got its name honestly. The land southwest of Houston was sugar plantation country in the 1800s, and in 1908 it became a company town for the Imperial Sugar Company. The refinery ran until 2003. Today Sugar Land is the largest city in Fort Bend County, about 19 miles southwest of downtown Houston, and it incorporated in 1959.

Interstate 69, also called US 59 or the Southwest Freeway, cuts diagonally through the city and carries most commuters toward Houston. State Highway 6, US 90A and the Grand Parkway handle the rest. Houston Methodist Sugar Land Hospital, Fluor and Schlumberger are among the big employers. Sugar Land Town Square is the city's downtown-style center, the Smart Financial Centre hosts concerts, and Constellation Field is home to the Sugar Land Space Cowboys. The University of Houston at Sugar Land and a branch of the Houston Museum of Natural Science are here too.

What it's like to live in Sugar Land

Sugar Land sits about 20 miles from downtown Houston, in the Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro. 107,726 people lived here in 2025, down 3.0% from 2020. It is suburban.

Places locals use include Sugar Land Memorial Park, Sugarland Memorial Park Running Trails and Fort Bend County Libraries.

The median age is 42.8, 37% of households have children, 80% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $136,217 a year (74% higher than the Texas median).

  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 116 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Sugar Land

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Sugar Land is $1,957 a month, 39% higher than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $78,000 a year. Right now 52% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,261 for a one-bedroom, $1,497 for a two-bedroom and $2,017 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $430,200, 52% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $2,355 before tax, or $2,965 with property tax. That is $1,008 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Sugar Land pays $7,320 a year, 73% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Sugar Land safe?

In 2025, Sugar Land Police Department reported 80 violent crimes and 1,339 property crimes. That works out to 73 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 81% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,222 property crimes per 100,000, 41% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime has been fairly steady since 2021.

Crime rates describe the whole area a police department covers. Some streets are much quieter than the average and some are busier, so look at the neighborhood grades and visit at different times of day.

Schools in Sugar Land

Sugar Land has 25 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 92% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Fort Bend ISD, Lamar CISD and Harmony Public Schools - Houston West.

Top-rated campuses include Commonwealth Elementary, Fort Settlement Middle and Bess Campbell Elementary. The average rating score across all campuses is 89 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
