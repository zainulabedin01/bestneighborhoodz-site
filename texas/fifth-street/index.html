What it's like to live in Fifth Street

Fifth Street sits about 16 miles from downtown Houston, in the Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro. About 1,453 people live here. It is suburban.

The median age is 17.6, 82% of households have children, 65% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $29,150 a year (63% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Fifth Street

  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 1.8 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.

Cons of living in Fifth Street

  • Higher poverty. 49% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Long commutes. The average trip to work takes 32 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Many adults uninsured. 31% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes, tornadoes and heat waves as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 112 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Fifth Street

Fifth Street renters pay a median of $1,307 a month, 7% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $52,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 82% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,261 for a one-bedroom, $1,497 for a two-bedroom and $2,017 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $52,800, 81% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $289 before tax, or $869 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.
