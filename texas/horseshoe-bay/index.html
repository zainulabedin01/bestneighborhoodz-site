What it's like to live in Horseshoe Bay

Horseshoe Bay is a town in Llano County. 5,339 people lived here in 2025, up 25.5% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is small-town and rural in feel.

The median age is 64.6, 10% of households have children, 81% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $96,794 a year (23% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Horseshoe Bay

  • Low violent crime. 73 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 81% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 22 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 26% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Horseshoe Bay

  • Homes are pricey for local incomes. The median home costs 5.7 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of river flooding, tornadoes and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Horseshoe Bay

Horseshoe Bay renters pay a median of $1,536 a month, 9% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $61,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 51% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $878 for a one-bedroom, $1,102 for a two-bedroom and $1,526 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $549,800, 94% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $3,009 before tax, or $3,426 with property tax. That is $1,890 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Horseshoe Bay pays $4,996 a year, 18% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
