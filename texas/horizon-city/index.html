What it's like to live in Horizon City

Horizon City sits about 18 miles from downtown El Paso, in the El Paso metro. 25,118 people lived here in 2025, up 11.8% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is suburban, with mostly newer homes (the median home was built in 2004).

Local spots people know include Golden Eagle Park, Horizon Park and Desmond Corcoran Park.

The median age is 29.5, 55% of households have children, 85% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $65,737 a year (16% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Horizon City

  • Low violent crime. 99 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 75% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.6 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.

Cons of living in Horizon City

  • Higher unemployment. 9.1% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Higher poverty. 22% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Many adults uninsured. 32% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hail and winter storms as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 117 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Horizon City

Horizon City renters pay a median of $1,280 a month, 9% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $51,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 72% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $969 for a one-bedroom, $1,146 for a two-bedroom and $1,559 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $170,200, 40% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $932 before tax, or $1,298 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Horizon City pays $4,399 a year, about the same as the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Schools in Horizon City

Horizon City has 10 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 70% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Socorro ISD, Clint ISD and Idea Public Schools.

Top-rated campuses include Col John O Ensor Middle, Horizon Heights Elementary and Horizon High School. The average rating score across all campuses is 84 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.

Daily life in Horizon City

Everyday places mapped inside Horizon City, from Overture Maps. Well-known spots include Golden Eagle Park, Horizon Park, Desmond Corcoran Park.

The average one-way commute is 29 minutes, 10% longer than the Texas average. 9% of workers work from home. Fewer than 3% walk, bike or take transit to work, so plan on driving for almost everything.

Downtown El Paso is about 18 miles away in a straight line. Many people who live in Horizon City work elsewhere in the metro, so test the drive at rush hour before you commit.

The median household income is $65,737, income per person is $22,788, unemployment is 9.1% and 21% of adults hold a bachelor's degree.
