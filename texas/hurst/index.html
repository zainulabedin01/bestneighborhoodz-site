What it's like to live in Hurst

Hurst sits about 24 miles from downtown Dallas, in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro. 38,974 people lived here in 2025, down 3.6% from 2020. It is densely built.

Places locals use include Hurst Community Park, Chisholm Park and Rickel Park.

The median age is 37.8, 29% of households have children, 58% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $72,210 a year (8% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Hurst

  • Strong public schools. 100% of the 9 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 173 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 55% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Plenty close to home. 8 grocery stores, 226 restaurants and 29 parks are mapped in town.

Cons of living in Hurst

  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in Hurst

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Hurst is $1,358 a month, about the same as the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $54,000 a year. Right now 52% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,481 for a one-bedroom, $1,745 for a two-bedroom and $2,284 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $315,200, 11% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,725 before tax, or $2,058 with property tax. That is $700 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Hurst pays $3,987 a year, 6% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Hurst safe?

In 2025, Hurst Police Department reported 68 violent crimes and 707 property crimes. That works out to 173 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 55% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,799 property crimes per 100,000, 14% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 15% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Schools in Hurst

Hurst has 9 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 100% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Grapevine-Colleyville ISD, Hurst-Euless-Bedford ISD and Birdville ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Grapevine-Colleyville Collegiate Academy at Tcc Ne, Hurst Hills Elementary and Bell High School. The average rating score across all campuses is 89 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
