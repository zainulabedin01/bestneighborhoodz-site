What it's like to live in Presidio

Presidio is a town in Presidio County. 2,909 people lived here in 2025, down 10.9% from 2020. It is spread out.

The median age is 29.0, 32% of households have children, 76% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $40,800 a year (48% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Presidio

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $433, 69% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 1.9 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 11 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Presidio

  • Higher unemployment. 10% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Higher poverty. 39% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Many adults uninsured. 42% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 189 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Presidio

Presidio renters pay a median of $433 a month, 69% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $17,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 12% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $858 for a one-bedroom, $1,078 for a two-bedroom and $1,393 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $78,800, 72% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $431 before tax, or $532 with property tax. That is $99 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Presidio pays $1,206 a year, 72% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
