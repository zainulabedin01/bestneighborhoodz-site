What it's like to live in Post

Post sits about 39 miles from downtown Lubbock, in the Lubbock metro. 3,494 people lived here in 2025, down 27.0% from 2020. It is spread out.

Places locals use include Post City Park, David Nichols Park and Post Public Library.

The median age is 34.5, 32% of households have children, 68% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $41,304 a year (47% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Post

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $822, 41% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 1.2 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 22 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Post

  • Weaker school ratings. None of the 3 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Higher unemployment. 8.6% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Many adults uninsured. 38% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, hail and wildfires as very high here.

Cost of living in Post

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Post is $822 a month, 41% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $33,000 a year. Right now 15% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $917 for a one-bedroom, $1,084 for a two-bedroom and $1,501 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $49,200, 83% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $269 before tax, or $365 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Post pays $1,149 a year, 73% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
