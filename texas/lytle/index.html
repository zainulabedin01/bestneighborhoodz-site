What it's like to live in Lytle

Lytle sits about 23 miles from downtown San Antonio, in the San Antonio-New Braunfels metro. 3,626 people lived here in 2025, up 24.4% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is small-town and rural in feel.

Places locals use include John Lott Municipal Park, Lytle park and Lytle Public Library.

The median age is 43.6, 32% of households have children, 84% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $70,685 a year (10% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Lytle

  • Low violent crime. 58 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 85% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,099, 22% lower than the Texas median.
  • Plenty close to home. 3 grocery stores, 27 restaurants and 4 parks are mapped in town.
  • Growing fast. The population grew 24% from 2020 to 2025, which usually means new homes, shops and jobs.

Cons of living in Lytle

  • Weaker school ratings. None of the 4 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hail and wildfires as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 120 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Lytle

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Lytle is $1,099 a month, 22% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $44,000 a year. Right now 79% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $982 for a one-bedroom, $1,069 for a two-bedroom and $1,404 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $187,500, 34% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,026 before tax, or $1,187 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Lytle pays $1,927 a year, 54% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
