About McAllen

Down at the southern tip of Texas, McAllen is one of the main cities of the Rio Grande Valley in Hidalgo County. Its city limits run south to the Rio Grande, right across from Reynosa, Mexico. Brownsville is about 60 miles to the southeast and San Antonio is about 238 miles north. The town was laid out in 1904 by the McAllen Townsite Company and named for John McAllen, one of its founders.

Most people get around on US 83 and Interstate 2, the main east to west route through the Valley, with US 281 and I-69C heading north. Two international bridges, the McAllen-Hidalgo-Reynosa bridge and the Anzalduas bridge, keep trade moving with Mexico. Farming once led the local economy. Now it runs on international trade, health care, retail and tourism, helped by the McAllen Foreign-Trade Zone, the first inland one in the country. Known spots include McAllen Miller International Airport, La Plaza Mall, the McAllen Convention Center and Quinta Mazatlan, a historic estate that doubles as a birding center.

What it's like to live in McAllen

McAllen is the anchor city of the McAllen-Edinburg-Mission metro area. 150,640 people lived here in 2025, up 5.9% from 2020. It is suburban.

Places locals use include Oval Park, Westside Park and Field and McAuliffe Park.

The median age is 33.5, 39% of households have children, 59% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $61,579 a year (22% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in McAllen

  • Strong public schools. 98% of the 46 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Low violent crime. 126 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 67% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,049, 25% lower than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 22 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Plenty close to home. 58 grocery stores, 999 restaurants and 96 parks are mapped in town.

Cons of living in McAllen

  • Higher poverty. 20% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Many adults uninsured. 30% of adults 18 to 64 have no health insurance, against 20% statewide.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes and hail as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 167 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in McAllen

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in McAllen is $1,049 a month, 25% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $42,000 a year. Right now 51% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $806 for a one-bedroom, $1,017 for a two-bedroom and $1,305 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $190,400, 33% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,042 before tax, or $1,316 with property tax. That is $267 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in McAllen pays $3,287 a year, 22% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is McAllen safe?

In 2025, McAllen Police Department reported 190 violent crimes and 2,139 property crimes. That works out to 126 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 67% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,422 property crimes per 100,000, 32% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 29% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Schools in McAllen

McAllen has 46 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 98% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by McAllen ISD, Idea Public Schools and Sharyland ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Achieve Early College High School, Idea Quest College Preparatory and Idea McAllen College Preparatory. The average rating score across all campuses is 89 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.

    Living in McAllen: common questions

    Is McAllen, TX a good place to live?

    We give McAllen a B+. It ranks 22nd of 75 Texas cities of 50,000+ people. It does best on schools and cost of living, and trails on jobs and income and health.

    Median rent in McAllen is $1,049 a month, 25% lower than the Texas median. The median home is worth $190,400, 33% lower than the Texas median.
