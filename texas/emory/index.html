What it's like to live in Emory

Emory is a town in Rains County. 1,413 people lived here in 2025, up 13.1% from 2020, so it is one of the faster-growing places in Texas. It is small-town and rural in feel.

Places locals use include Sandy Creek Park, Ruby Mckeowan Park and Emory Park.

The median age is 34.8, 34% of households have children, 48% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $36,500 a year (53% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Emory

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $940, 33% lower than the Texas median.

Cons of living in Emory

  • Homes are pricey for local incomes. The median home costs 6.0 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Higher poverty. 29% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.

Cost of living in Emory

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Emory is $940 a month, 33% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $38,000 a year. Right now 39% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $800 for a one-bedroom, $1,014 for a two-bedroom and $1,404 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $218,100, 23% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,194 before tax, or $1,408 with property tax. That is $468 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Emory pays $2,571 a year, 39% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

In 2025, Emory Police Department reported 4 violent crimes and 13 property crimes. That works out to 281 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 28% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 912 property crimes per 100,000, 56% lower than the state rate.
