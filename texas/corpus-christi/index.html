About Corpus Christi

Corpus Christi wraps around Corpus Christi Bay and Nueces Bay on the Texas Gulf Coast, with Padre Island and Mustang Island just to the east. It is the seat of Nueces County, about 130 miles southeast of San Antonio. Henry Lawrence Kinney started it as a trading post in 1839, and its Latin name means Body of Christ.

I-37 is the main highway in and out, linking the city to San Antonio, while SH 358, US 181 and SH 44 handle much of the local and regional driving. The Harbor Bridge and the JFK Memorial Causeway are the main crossings. Jobs center on the Port of Corpus Christi and its refineries, Naval Air Station Corpus Christi, the Corpus Christi Army Depot, Christus Spohn Health System and Texas A&M University Corpus Christi. Out on North Beach you will find the Texas State Aquarium and the USS Lexington Museum, and the downtown seawall is a favorite place to walk along the bay.

What it's like to live in Corpus Christi

Corpus Christi is the anchor city of the Corpus Christi metro area. 317,247 people lived here in 2025, down 0.3% from 2020.

Local spots people know include Bill Witt Dog Park, Oso Creek and Oso Bay Nature Trails.

The median age is 36.9, 32% of households have children, 58% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $67,394 a year (14% lower than the Texas median).

Cost of living in Corpus Christi

Corpus Christi renters pay a median of $1,292 a month, 8% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $52,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 52% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,176 for a one-bedroom, $1,421 for a two-bedroom and $1,855 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $210,400, 26% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,152 before tax, or $1,434 with property tax. That is $142 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Corpus Christi pays $3,387 a year, 20% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Corpus Christi safe?

In 2025, Corpus Christi Police Department reported 2,533 violent crimes and 8,834 property crimes. That works out to 799 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 106% higher than the Texas rate of 387, and 2,786 property crimes per 100,000, 34% higher than the state rate.

Violent crime has been fairly steady since 2021.

Crime rates describe the whole area a police department covers. Some streets are much quieter than the average and some are busier, so look at the neighborhood grades and visit at different times of day.

Schools in Corpus Christi

Top-rated campuses include Collegiate High School, Harold T Branch Academy for Career & Technical Ed and Windsor Park G/T. The average rating score across all campuses is 83 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

    Living in Corpus Christi: common questions
