What it's like to live in University Park

University Park sits about 4 miles from downtown Dallas, in the Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington metro. 25,323 people lived here in 2025, up 0.2% from 2020. It is densely built.

Local spots people know include Curtis Park, Caruth Park and Native Texas Park.

The median age is 29.4, 50% of households have children, 83% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $250,000+ a year (219% higher than the Texas median).

Pros of living in University Park

  • Strong public schools. 100% of the 5 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • High incomes. The median household earns $250,000+, 219% higher than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 17 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Plenty close to home. 3 grocery stores, 93 restaurants and 24 parks are mapped in town.
  • Healthier population. Only 6.3% of working-age adults lack health insurance.

Cons of living in University Park

  • Higher rent. Median rent is $3,272, 133% higher than the Texas median.
  • Homes are pricey for local incomes. The median home costs 7.4 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, heat waves and hail as very high here.

Cost of living in University Park

University Park renters pay a median of $3,272 a month, 133% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $131,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 48% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,536 for a one-bedroom, $1,807 for a two-bedroom and $2,275 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $1,861,800, 556% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $10,191 before tax, or $11,024 with property tax. That is $7,752 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in University Park pays $10,000+ a year, 136% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
