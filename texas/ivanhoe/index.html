What it's like to live in Ivanhoe

Ivanhoe is a town in Tyler County. 1,376 people lived here in 2025, up 3.5% from 2020. It is small-town and rural in feel.

The median age is 46.5, 31% of households have children, 78% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $52,054 a year (34% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Ivanhoe

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,170, 17% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 1.9 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.

Cons of living in Ivanhoe

  • Higher unemployment. 8.5% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Higher poverty. 33% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes, tornadoes and wildfires as very high here.

Cost of living in Ivanhoe

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Ivanhoe is $1,170 a month, 17% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $47,000 a year. Right now 48% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $854 for a one-bedroom, $1,117 for a two-bedroom and $1,331 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $98,200, 65% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $538 before tax, or $670 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Ivanhoe pays $1,588 a year, 62% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
