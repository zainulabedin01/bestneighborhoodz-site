What it's like to live in Port Arthur

Port Arthur sits about 21 miles from downtown Beaumont, in the Beaumont-Port Arthur metro. 55,804 people lived here in 2025, down 0.4% from 2020. It is spread out.

Places locals use include Walter Umphrey Pier, Golden Triangle Veterans Memorial Park and Gates Memorial Library.

The median age is 35.5, 36% of households have children, 58% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $46,354 a year (41% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Port Arthur

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,021, 27% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.3 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.

Cons of living in Port Arthur

  • Weaker school ratings. Only 26% of the 19 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Higher violent crime. 596 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 54% higher than the Texas rate.
  • Higher unemployment. 8.4% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Higher poverty. 29% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.

Cost of living in Port Arthur

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Port Arthur is $1,021 a month, 27% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $41,000 a year. Right now 58% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $986 for a one-bedroom, $1,218 for a two-bedroom and $1,588 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $107,400, 62% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $588 before tax, or $715 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Port Arthur pays $1,521 a year, 64% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Port Arthur safe?

In 2025, Port Arthur Police Department reported 332 violent crimes and 743 property crimes. That works out to 596 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 54% higher than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,333 property crimes per 100,000, 36% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 23% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Schools in Port Arthur

Port Arthur has 19 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 26% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Bob Hope School, Port Arthur ISD and Sabine Pass ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Bob Hope High School and Bob Hope School. The average rating score across all campuses is 70 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
