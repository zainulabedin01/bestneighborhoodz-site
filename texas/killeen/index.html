What it's like to live in Killeen

Killeen is the anchor city of the Killeen-Temple metro area. 161,883 people lived here in 2025, up 5.7% from 2020. It is suburban.

Places locals use include Long Branch Park, Lions Club Park and Copper Mountain Library.

The median age is 30.3, 38% of households have children, 49% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $60,977 a year (22% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Killeen

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,208, 14% lower than the Texas median.

Cons of living in Killeen

  • Weaker school ratings. Only 22% of the 32 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Higher unemployment. 9.7% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Errands mean a drive. Compared with similar Texas places, fewer homes here are within a mile of a grocery store, pharmacy, park or clinic.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, hail and winter storms as very high here.

Cost of living in Killeen

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in Killeen is $1,208 a month, 14% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $48,000 a year. Right now 53% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $927 for a one-bedroom, $1,138 for a two-bedroom and $1,576 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $215,400, 24% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,179 before tax, or $1,452 with property tax. That is $244 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Killeen pays $3,275 a year, 23% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Killeen safe?

In 2025, Killeen Police Department reported 808 violent crimes and 2,668 property crimes. That works out to 498 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 28% higher than the Texas rate of 387, and 1,643 property crimes per 100,000, 21% lower than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 17% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Crime rates describe the whole area a police department covers. Some streets are much quieter than the average and some are busier, so look at the neighborhood grades and visit at different times of day.

Schools in Killeen

Killeen has 32 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 22% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Killeen ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Ira Cross Jr Elementary. The average rating score across all campuses is 73 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
