What it's like to live in Coleman

Coleman is a town in Coleman County. 3,991 people lived here in 2025, up 1.8% from 2020. It is small-town and rural in feel, with an older housing stock (the median home dates to 1958).

Local spots people know include Coleman City Park, Memory Lake Park and Coleman Public Library.

The median age is 43.6, 24% of households have children, 73% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $34,613 a year (56% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Coleman

  • Low violent crime. 98 violent crimes per 100,000 people in 2025, 75% lower than the Texas rate.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $713, 49% lower than the Texas median.
  • Homes are affordable for local incomes. The median home costs 2.1 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.

Cons of living in Coleman

  • Weaker school ratings. Only 33% of the 3 rated campuses earn an A or B from the state.
  • Higher unemployment. 12% of the labor force is out of work, against 5.3% statewide.
  • Higher poverty. 25% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Long commutes. The average trip to work takes 36 minutes, against 27 across Texas.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, hail and wildfires as very high here.

Cost of living in Coleman

Coleman renters pay a median of $713 a month, 49% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $29,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 46% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $825 for a one-bedroom, $1,079 for a two-bedroom and $1,286 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $71,900, 75% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $394 before tax, or $477 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Coleman pays $1,000 a year, 76% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
