    Living in Cedar Park: common questions

    Is Cedar Park, TX a good place to live?

    We give Cedar Park an A. It ranks 11th of 75 Texas cities of 50,000+ people. It does best on home ownership and education levels, and trails on getting around and cost of living. It is a strong pick for families and retirees.

    How much does it cost to live in Cedar Park?

    Median rent in Cedar Park is $1,846 a month, 32% higher than the Texas median. The median home is worth $513,600, 81% higher than the Texas median. A median home costs 4.0x the median household income, compared with 3.6x across Texas. With 20% down at a 7.28% rate, the monthly payment on a median home is about $3,552, including property tax.

    Is Cedar Park safe?

    In 2025, Cedar Park Police Department reported 91 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 77% lower than the Texas rate. Property crime was 1,223 per 100,000, 41% lower than the Texas rate.

    Are the schools good in Cedar Park?

    We found 17 rated public school campuses inside Cedar Park. 77% are rated A or B by the Texas Education Agency. Its schools score is 77 of 100 compared with other Texas cities of 50,000+ people.

    What are the best neighborhoods in Cedar Park?

    Based on our scores, the top-rated areas in Cedar Park are Southwest Cedar Park, Anderson Mill West and Avery Ranch. We score 6 areas in the city.

    Does Cedar Park flood?

    FEMA rates river flooding risk in Cedar Park as low, and coastal flooding as not rated. Hazards rated high or very high here: hail, wildfires and winter storms. Always check the flood map for a specific address before you buy.

    What is the weather like in Cedar Park?

    Summers in Cedar Park peak at about 96°F in July, and January nights fall to about 38°F. Expect around 114 days a year at 90°F or hotter, and 37 inches of rain.

    How long is the commute in Cedar Park?

    The average one-way commute is 25 min, 4% lower than the Texas average. About 30% of workers work from home. It is about 15 miles from downtown Austin.
