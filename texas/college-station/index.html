About College Station

College Station grew up around Texas A&M University in the Brazos Valley. It sits in Brazos County, about 83 miles northwest of Houston and 87 miles east of Austin, next to the city of Bryan. The Houston and Texas Central Railway came through in the 1860s, and the college site was chosen in 1871. Texas A&M opened in 1876 as the first public college in Texas, but the city itself was not incorporated until 1938.

Life here moves on the school calendar. Texas A&M is the largest employer by a wide margin, with St. Joseph Health and Sanderson Farms also among the big names in the area. State Highway 6, the Earl Rudder Freeway, is the main north to south road. University Drive, SH 40 and SH 47 handle much of the cross-town traffic. On fall Saturdays the town fills up for football at Kyle Field. Other well-known places include the George Bush Presidential Library, the Northgate district of bars and restaurants by campus, and Easterwood Airport.

What it's like to live in College Station

College Station is the anchor city of the College Station-Bryan metro area. 127,472 people lived here in 2025, up 5.8% from 2020. It is suburban.

Places locals use include Lick Creek Park, Southern Oaks Park and Wolf Penn Park.

The median age is 22.9, 24% of households have children, 35% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $50,900 a year (35% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in College Station

  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,194, 15% lower than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 17 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in College Station

  • Homes are pricey for local incomes. The median home costs 6.8 times the median household income, against 3.6 times across Texas.
  • Higher poverty. 29% of people live below the poverty line, against 14% across Texas.
  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 117 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in College Station

Rent is the cost most newcomers feel first. The median rent in College Station is $1,194 a month, 15% lower than the Texas median of $1,403. To keep that rent at 30% of income, a household needs to earn about $48,000 a year. Right now 65% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,057 for a one-bedroom, $1,151 for a two-bedroom and $1,594 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $346,700, 22% higher than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,898 before tax, or $2,375 with property tax. That is $1,181 a month more than the median rent, so renting is the cheaper monthly choice unless you plan to stay for years.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in College Station pays $5,730 a year, 35% higher than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Top-rated campuses include Forest Ridge, Iltexas Aggieland High School and River Bend Elementary. The average rating score across all campuses is 84 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.
