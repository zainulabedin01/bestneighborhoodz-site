About Spring

Roughly 20 miles north of downtown Houston along Interstate 45, Spring is an unincorporated community in north Harris County, counted as a census-designated place. It began as a railroad town after the tracks came through in 1871, with German farmers already working the land along Spring Creek. The rail roundhouse moved to Houston in 1923 and the town shrank, until Houston's suburbs reached it in the 1970s.

I-45 is the main road for most people, and the Hardy Toll Road runs alongside it down to Houston, with a branch to Bush Intercontinental Airport. ExxonMobil's large corporate campus, opened in 2014, is now the company's headquarters. Schools are split among Spring ISD, Klein ISD and Conroe ISD depending on the address, and Lone Star College serves the area. Old Town Spring, the original town center near Spring-Cypress and Hardy roads, is full of small shops in restored buildings. Pundt Park and Mercer Arboretum and Botanic Gardens are nearby green spaces.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org, tshaonline.org, en.wikipedia.org

What it's like to live in Spring

Spring sits about 19 miles from downtown Houston, in the Houston-Pasadena-The Woodlands metro. About 68,580 people live here. It is suburban.

Local spots people know include Dennis Johnston Park, Pundt Park and John Pundt Park.

The median age is 32.7, 39% of households have children, 74% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $88,997 a year (13% higher than the Texas median).

Cost of living in Spring

Spring renters pay a median of $1,711 a month, 22% higher than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $68,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 39% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,261 for a one-bedroom, $1,497 for a two-bedroom and $2,017 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $231,500, 18% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,267 before tax, or $1,603 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but property taxes are among the highest in the country. The median homeowner in Spring pays $4,027 a year, 5% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.
