What it's like to live in Longview

Longview is the anchor city of the Longview metro area. 83,236 people lived here in 2025, up 1.8% from 2020. It is spread out.

Local spots people know include Lear Park Athletic Complex, Paul Boorman Park and Trailhead and Lear Park.

The median age is 35.3, 34% of households have children, 55% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $63,340 a year (19% lower than the Texas median).

Pros of living in Longview

  • Strong public schools. 96% of the 22 rated campuses here earn an A or B from the Texas Education Agency.
  • Lower rent. Median rent is $1,101, 22% lower than the Texas median.
  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 21 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

Cons of living in Longview

  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of tornadoes, heat waves and hail as very high here.
  • Long, hot summers. Expect about 112 days a year at 90°F or hotter.

Cost of living in Longview

Longview renters pay a median of $1,101 a month, 22% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $44,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 51% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $1,041 for a one-bedroom, $1,232 for a two-bedroom and $1,611 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $202,600, 29% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $1,109 before tax, or $1,310 with property tax. That is $209 a month more than the median rent, a gap that can be worth it if you plan to stay a while.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Longview pays $2,412 a year, 43% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Longview safe?

In 2025, Longview Police Department reported 291 violent crimes and 1,793 property crimes. That works out to 346 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 11% lower than the Texas rate of 387, and 2,131 property crimes per 100,000, about the same as the state rate.

Violent crime has been fairly steady since 2021.

Crime rates describe the whole area a police department covers. Some streets are much quieter than the average and some are busier, so look at the neighborhood grades and visit at different times of day.

Schools in Longview

Longview has 22 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 96% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Longview ISD, Ut Tyler University Academy and Hallsville ISD.

Top-rated campuses include Hudson Elementary, Ut Tyler University Academy at Longview and Foster Middle. The average rating score across all campuses is 88 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
