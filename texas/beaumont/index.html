About Beaumont

Beaumont sits on the west bank of the Neches River, about 85 miles east of Houston on Interstate 10. It is the seat of Jefferson County and one corner of the Golden Triangle, along with Port Arthur and Orange. Before 1901 it was a lumber and rice town. Then the Spindletop gusher blew in nearby, starting the Texas oil boom and changing the place for good.

Oil is still the backbone here. The ExxonMobil Beaumont Refinery is the biggest private employer, and the Port of Beaumont moves cargo up the Neches ship channel. Lamar University, Baptist Hospitals of Southeast Texas and CHRISTUS St. Elizabeth are other major employers. I-10 is the main road through town and the route to Houston and Louisiana. The Spindletop Gladys City Boomtown Museum, the Fire Museum of Texas and Tyrrell Park with its Cattail Marsh are local spots, and the Big Thicket woods are a short drive north.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org, tshaonline.org

What it's like to live in Beaumont

Beaumont is the anchor city of the Beaumont-Port Arthur metro area. 112,967 people lived here in 2025, down 1.8% from 2020. It is spread out.

Local spots people know include Tyrrell Park and Cattail Marsh and R C Miller Memorial Library.

The median age is 35.3, 31% of households have children, 55% of homes are owned by the people living in them and the typical household earns $56,997 a year (27% lower than the Texas median).

  • Short commutes. The average trip to work takes 20 minutes, against 27 across Texas.

  • Natural hazard risk. FEMA rates the risk of hurricanes and tornadoes as very high here.

Cost of living in Beaumont

Beaumont renters pay a median of $1,121 a month, 20% lower than the statewide median. The usual rule is to spend no more than 30% of income on rent, which means earning around $45,000 a year for a median unit. Right now 54% of renters here pay more than 30% of their income on rent.

HUD sets fair market rents for this area at $986 for a one-bedroom, $1,218 for a two-bedroom and $1,588 for a three-bedroom (fiscal year 2027). These figures include utilities and describe a modest unit, so newer apartments often ask for more.

The median home is worth $166,400, 41% lower than the Texas median. With 20% down and a 30-year loan at 7.28%, the monthly payment comes to about $911 before tax, or $1,130 with property tax. That is close to the median rent, so buying can make sense if you plan to stay.

Texas has no state income tax, but it leans on property taxes instead, though they are lighter here than in much of the state. The median homeowner in Beaumont pays $2,627 a year, 38% lower than the Texas median of $4,232. Budget for it, along with homeowners insurance, which our numbers do not include.

Is Beaumont safe?

In 2025, Beaumont Police Department reported 1,065 violent crimes and 3,334 property crimes. That works out to 948 violent crimes per 100,000 people, 145% higher than the Texas rate of 387, and 2,966 property crimes per 100,000, 42% higher than the state rate.

Violent crime is down about 13% since 2021, based on the yearly rates below.

Schools in Beaumont

Beaumont has 26 public school campuses with a 2025 state rating inside the city, and 31% of them earn an A or B. Most students here are served by Texas College Preparatory Academies, Beaumont ISD and Ehrhart School.

Top-rated campuses include Ignite Community School - Beaumont, Beaumont ISD Early College High School and Ehrhart School. The average rating score across all campuses is 72 out of 100, against a Texas median of 81.

Texas districts usually assign students by attendance zone rather than the closest campus, so check the zone for a specific address with the district before you sign a lease or buy.
